How you play

Two hands, like a real guitar.

Your left hand works the neck, your right hand strums. Every move is something a real guitarist does.

Left hand

The fret remote

Tilt front or backChanges the chord row — up, level or down.
Slide left or rightMoves up the neck — chords shift and ringing strings glide.
Touch pads 1, 2, 3Pick the chord, like pressing the strings down.
Right hand

The strum remote

Small flickPlays just one or two strings.
Full swingAll six strings, one after another — like a real pick.
Down and upDown starts on the thick strings, up on the thin ones.
Three sounds

Campfire to heavy metal.

Real recorded guitar strings, played through a wooden body or a guitar amp. Switch any time — even mid-song.

Acoustic

Warm & bright

Steel strings and a wooden body. Perfect for singing along.

Rock

Crunchy amp

An electric guitar pushing a hot amp. Big, punchy chords.

Heavy Metal

High gain

Power chords and palm-muted chugs through a wall of speakers.

What's real right now.

AirKords is a working prototype that's still growing. Here's an honest look at where it is.

Working today
  • Motion sensing and strumming in the air
  • Real guitar sound in three styles
  • The AI band that follows your beat
  • Recording and publishing songs
Being built now
  • The two-remote guitar with touch frets
  • Six touch strips for full chord shapes
  • A 3D-printed body for each remote
Oct 2026

Two hands

AirKords becomes two remotes: a fret remote with a touch slider for chords, and a strum remote for the strumming hand — just like a real guitar.

Sep 4, 2026

First public demo

AirKords went in front of a live crowd for the first time, next to an amp at a tech meetup — strumming the air and hearing it through real speakers.

Source: @CaptVenk on X
Sep 3, 2026

First strum, on screen

The first on-camera test: motion from the handheld controller driving a guitar on screen in real time, built on an ESP32 and a gyroscope sensor.

Source: @CaptVenk on X
Sep 3, 2026

The idea behind the name

AirKords started life as AiroMote — a motion controller for games, music and workouts that reads movement from the device itself, not a camera. Guitar became the focus, and the name followed.

Source: @CaptVenk on X
